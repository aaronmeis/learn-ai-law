# 2 · Socratic examiner prompts

Be quizzed, pushed, and gap-checked. This is the 200→300 engine — the point is
to find what you *think* you know but can't defend.

**Model: Opus 5** for Weeks 2–6 and capstone prep. **Curriculum fit:** end of
every pillar week; all of Week 6.

---

## 2.1 — Cold-call examiner

```
You are a law-school examiner running a Socratic cold-call on me. Topic:
«topic / week». My target rung is «200 / 300».

Rules:
- Ask ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer. Do not answer for me.
- Start concrete, then push to the edge case, then to the policy/unsettled layer.
- When I'm vague, don't accept it — ask "which authority?" or "what's the
  counter-argument?"
- When I'm wrong, don't correct immediately — ask a question that exposes the
  error.
- After 6–8 exchanges, stop and give me: what I had solid, what was shaky, what
  I got wrong, and the 2 readings that would close the gap.

Begin.
```

---

## 2.2 — Steelman / attack my position

```
I'm going to state a position on «issue». Your job:
1. Steelman it — the strongest version, with the best authority.
2. Then attack it — the strongest counter, with authority.
3. Tell me where a court or regulator has actually come down, and how confident
   you are that's still current.
4. Give me the version of my position that would survive pushback from counsel.

My position: «...»
```

---

## 2.3 — "Where am I overconfident?"

```
Quiz me to find where my understanding of «pillar / topic» is thinner than I
think it is. Ask 10 rapid questions ranging from definitional to genuinely
contested. Don't tell me the answers as we go.

At the end, score each answer (solid / shaky / wrong / bluffing), and rank the
top 3 gaps by how much they'd embarrass me in a room with a lawyer. Map each
gap to a specific reading from the curriculum.
```

---

## 2.4 — Hypothetical escalator

```
Give me a hypothetical on «topic» and escalate it in steps. Start with a clean
fact pattern; after each answer I give, change one fact to make it harder or
flip the outcome. Keep going for ~6 rounds.

I want to feel where the legal line actually is — which fact is doing the work.
After the last round, name the doctrinal test or factor that each fact was
probing.
```

---

## 2.5 — Mock "meet with counsel" (capstone rehearsal)

```
Play a skeptical senior in-house counsel. I'm the AI architect presenting the
legal-risk map for a (fictional / public-exemplar) AI deployment: «paste your
issue-spotter output or risk map».

Push back like counsel would: challenge my issue-spotting, ask what authority
I'm relying on, probe the clauses I say I'd redline, and test whether I know the
limits of my own analysis. Interrupt if I overclaim.

Run ~20 minutes of back-and-forth. Then step out of role and give me:
- Where I held up.
- Where counsel would have lost confidence in me.
- 3 things to tighten before doing this for real.
```

---

## 2.6 — Explain-it-back check (Feynman)

```
I'll explain «concept» to you as if you're a smart colleague with no legal
background. Listen, then:
- Point out every place I was imprecise, hand-wavy, or wrong.
- Ask the 3 follow-up questions a lawyer would ask that I didn't pre-empt.
- Give me the 2-sentence version that is both accurate and plain.

My explanation: «...»
```
