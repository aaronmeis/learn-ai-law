## Title
Intellectual Property & AI: what's settled, what's not

## Column 1 — Training data & fair use
- The question: is copying works to train a model fair use under §107, or infringement? No appellate ruling as of 2026
- Four factors; factor 4 (market harm) has been decisive
- EU has a statutory TDM exception + machine-readable opt-out; the US has only fair use
- Acquisition ≠ use: how the corpus was obtained is its own risk (pirated copies)

## Column 2 — Authorship & outputs
- Human-authorship requirement: pure machine output can't be registered (*Thaler v. Perlmutter*, cert. denied 2026)
- Prompts alone ≠ authorship (Copyright Office Part 2)
- Protectable: human edits + selection/arrangement (*Zarya of the Dawn*)
- Output-side risk: substantial similarity / regurgitation (*Authors Guild v. OpenAI*)

## Column 3 — Patents, trade secret, licensing
- Inventor must be a natural person (*Thaler v. Vidal*)
- USPTO 28 Nov 2025 guidance: drops the Pannu test for AI, treats AI as a tool
- §101: generic ML on a new data field is ineligible (*Recentive v. Fox*)
- Trade secret = the practical mode for weights/pipelines/prompts (DTSA); lost if disclosed
- Open weights ≠ open source (OSAID 1.0): Llama-style licenses add MAU caps + no-compete-training + AUP

## Bottom timeline — the 2025 training rulings
- Feb 2025 *Thomson Reuters v. Ross* — fair use REJECTED, all four factors (on appeal, 3rd Cir.)
- Jun 2025 *Bartz v. Anthropic* — training transformative; pirated copies separately infringing; ~$1.5B settlement (Jul 2026)
- Jun 2025 *Kadrey v. Meta* — training transformative; "not a ruling that AI training is lawful"; dilution record undeveloped
